Privacy Policy

Last updated September 20, 2026

Unsubscribely is committed to protecting your privacy. This Privacy Policy explains how we collect, use, and safeguard your information when you use our services. By using Unsubscribely, you agree to the terms outlined in this policy. This page explains what is collected, how it is used, and what you can do to protect your privacy. Unsubscribely is a free and open-source tool that helps you unsubscribe from unwanted emails and newsletters. It is designed to be simple, fast, and privacy-focused. Unlike other services, Unsubscribely does not require you to sign up or provide any personal information. You can simply enter your email address and start cleaning up your inbox right away.

  • Only sender details are used. Unsubscribely uses the sender and the unsubscribe header of your most recent emails. It doesn't use message bodies or attachments.
  • Nothing goes into a database. Connection details are held temporarily in the server's memory or in your browser tab, and there is no account to create.

Your email data does pass through Unsubscribely's server on its way to your screen. It is processed there and not saved, and the sections below explain how.

Who runs Unsubscribely

Unsubscribely is a free, open-source project (GPL-3.0) made by Pancakse. It is currently in beta.

This policy covers the hosted version of Unsubscribely. If you run your own copy from GitHub, whoever runs that copy is responsible for the data it handles, and this policy doesn't apply to them. The source code is public, so you can check anything written here.

What Unsubscribely reads

What happens depends on how you connect your inbox. In every case, Unsubscribely only scans a limited number of your most recent emails.

Provider How you connect What is read Scan limit
Gmail Google sign-in (OAuth) The From and List-Unsubscribe headers of each message 100 most recent emails
Outlook Microsoft sign-in (OAuth) The sender and message headers, of which only the sender and List-Unsubscribe are used 100 most recent emails
iCloud Mail, Yahoo Mail Email address and app-specific password over IMAP The sender and the List-Unsubscribe header of each message in your inbox About 150 most recent emails

The permissions that Google and Microsoft offer are broader than what Unsubscribely needs. For example, Google's read-only permission technically allows reading whole emails. Unsubscribely's code asks only for the sender and unsubscribe headers, and you can verify that in the source.

What it does with that

  • Builds your sender list. It groups your recent emails by sender, counts them, and checks whether each sender included an unsubscribe link. The result is shown on your dashboard.
  • Unsubscribes when you click. When you press Unsubscribe, Unsubscribely's server sends a one-click unsubscribe request to the web address the sender included in their email. If a sender only provides an email address for unsubscribing, that address is handed back to your browser. Unsubscribely doesn't send email on your behalf.
  • Doesn't touch your mailbox. It doesn't delete, move, label, or change any of your messages.

Your data isn't used for profiling, advertising, or training AI models, and it isn't sold.

What is stored

Unsubscribely has no database. Here is everything that is held, and where.

Gmail and Outlook

After you sign in, the access details Google or Microsoft give the app are kept in a session on the server. For Gmail these include a refresh token, which lets the app keep access without asking you to sign in again. For Outlook, only a short-lived access token is kept.

Sessions are held in the server's memory, not written to disk, and they disappear when the server restarts. Your sender list is built when the dashboard loads, sent to your browser, and not saved on the server.

iCloud and Yahoo

Your email address and app-specific password are sent to the server, used to open a single connection to your mail provider, and then discarded. They aren't saved or logged. The resulting sender list is kept in your browser's session storage so the dashboard can show it (see below).

Logs

The server writes error messages to its console for debugging. It doesn't log your inbox contents or your password. The hosting provider that runs the server may keep ordinary request logs, such as IP addresses and timestamps, as part of normal operation. Unsubscribely doesn't control or read those.

Testing mode

Gmail and Outlook access is currently limited to approved testers. If you're a tester, your account's email address is on an allowlist inside the project's Google Cloud or Microsoft Azure console, which only the developer can see.

Cookies and browser storage

  • Session cookie. Connecting Gmail or Outlook sets a cookie that links your browser to its session on the server. It is needed for the app to work and is not used for tracking. It is not set if you only use iCloud or Yahoo.
  • Session storage. For iCloud and Yahoo, your sender list is saved in your browser's sessionStorage. It is cleared when you close the tab.
  • Nothing else. There are no analytics, advertising, or tracking cookies, and no third-party scripts or fonts.

Who else is involved

Unsubscribely doesn't share your data for marketing or sell it. These parties do take part in making it work:

  • Google or Microsoft, when you sign in and when Unsubscribely fetches your sender information. Their own privacy policies apply to that.
  • Your mail provider (such as Apple or Yahoo), when you connect over IMAP.
  • The sender you unsubscribe from. When you click Unsubscribe, the request goes to the sender's own server. Unsubscribe links often contain a code that identifies your address, so the sender will know that address unsubscribed. The request comes from Unsubscribely's server, not your device.
  • The hosting provider that runs Unsubscribely's server.

Data may also be disclosed if the law requires it, though the app holds very little beyond a live session.

Google user data

Unsubscribely's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Specifically, for Gmail data:

  • Permissions requested. gmail.readonly is used to read sender and unsubscribe headers. gmail.modify is also requested, but Unsubscribely does not currently use it to change, delete, or send anything.
  • Google user data is used only to show you your senders and to let you unsubscribe from them.
  • It isn't sold, and it isn't used or transferred for advertising, including retargeting or interest-based ads.
  • It isn't used to develop, improve, or train AI or machine-learning models.
  • Nobody reads your data unless you ask for help with a specific problem, it is needed for security or abuse investigations, or the law requires it.

Your choices

  • Revoke access at the source. Remove Unsubscribely from your Google account's third-party access or your Microsoft account's app permissions. This makes any tokens Unsubscribely holds useless straight away.
  • iCloud and Yahoo. Delete the app-specific password in your Apple or Yahoo account settings once you're done. It's a good habit even though Unsubscribely doesn't keep it.
  • Clear browser data. Closing the tab clears session storage. Clearing your cookies removes the link to your server session.
  • Ask for your session to be cleared. There isn't a sign-out button yet. To have a session removed sooner than the next server restart, use the contact details below.

Security

Access tokens and passwords stay on the server and are not sent back to your browser. Unsubscribely connects to Google, Microsoft, and IMAP servers over encrypted connections.

This is a beta project run by one developer, and no system is perfectly secure. Use an app-specific password rather than your main password wherever a provider offers one, and revoke access when you're finished.

Children

Unsubscribely isn't directed at children under 13, and it doesn't knowingly collect information from them.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, such as the EU, the UK, or California, you may have the right to access, correct, delete, or restrict the use of your personal data. Because Unsubscribely keeps almost nothing beyond a live session, using the choices above covers most of this. For anything else, get in touch and the request will be handled.
